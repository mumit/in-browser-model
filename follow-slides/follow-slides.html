The pipeline

  • Ears. Whisper transcribes your voice in 3-second chunks, entirely in this tab. Quiet chunks are skipped, and common Whisper “hallucinations” on silence (“Thank you.”) are filtered out.
  • Matching. MiniLM turns each slide (and each bullet on it) into an embedding, a list of 384 numbers that captures meaning. Your last few seconds of speech get the same treatment, and cosine similarity scores how close you are to each slide.
  • Calling the cue. It only considers the next slide (and the previous one, if going back is on). The next slide has to beat the current one by a margin, twice in a row, before it advances. After a move, the listening window resets so old words don't drag it along.

Voice commands

Say “next slide”, “previous slide” or “go back”, or “go to slide 4”. These always work, even with auto-advance off.

Writing a deck that follows well

  • Give neighbouring slides different vocabulary. Two slides about “performance” will fight each other.
  • Add cue: lines with the phrases you naturally use when you reach that slide, like cue: let me show you a quick demo.
  • Rehearse once with the simulate box, then with your voice, and tune Eagerness.

Talk notes

Everything you say is filed under the slide that was showing, with the time spent on each. Copy it as Markdown for speaker notes or a write-up. It stays in this browser tab only.

Privacy

With Whisper selected, audio never leaves the laptop. The Chrome speech service option is faster but sends audio to Google; it's there as a fallback for slower machines.
